# Anaheim Ducks: 2026 Offseason Before/After Research Brief

**Coverage window:** End of the 2025–26 season through August 6, 2026  
**Target comparison:** The team that finished the 2026 playoffs vs. the projected team for Game 1 of 2026–27  
**Editorial status:** Pre-camp. Opening-night roster is not final.

## Executive summary

The Ducks are not coming off another rebuilding year. They went **43–33–6 (92 points), finished third in the Pacific, ended a seven-season playoff drought, upset Edmonton in six games, and then lost to Vegas in six in the second round**. The useful story is therefore not “rebuild to contender,” but “a young playoff team chooses which parts of its breakthrough roster to keep.”

Anaheim retained the heart of the young core—Leo Carlsson, Pavel Mintyukov, Jackson LaCombe, Lukas Dostal and Beckett Sennecke—but radically changed the supporting cast. Center Mason McTavish and defenseman Olen Zellweger were traded at the draft. The entire veteran defense group from the playoff roster—captain Radko Gudas, Jacob Trouba and deadline acquisition John Carlson—also departed. A.J. Greer, Jeff Malott and Nick Jensen are the principal NHL additions. The Ducks matched Philadelphia’s five-year, $90 million offer sheet to Carlsson, the defining transaction of the summer.

The roster remains unfinished. Leading scorer Cutter Gauthier is an unsigned restricted free agent as of this brief; Troy Terry is listed injured (hip); the captaincy is vacant; and the sixth-defenseman/extra-forward jobs will be decided in camp.

## Recommended page thesis

> **The breakthrough core stayed. The veteran shell did not.** After winning a playoff round for the first time since 2017, Anaheim spent the summer paying its franchise center, trading two prominent young players for flexibility and futures, and replacing four regular defensemen with cheaper veterans and internal prospects.

Use this as the narrative spine. It is more precise than calling the summer either a teardown or a conventional “win-now” offseason.

## Coaching and front office

- **No Anaheim head-coaching change.** Joel Quenneville returns after going 43–33–6 and winning his 1,000th NHL game during the season.
- Current listed NHL staff: Quenneville (head coach); Tim Army, Jay Woodcroft, Ryan McGill and Andrew Brewer (assistants); Peter Budaj (goaltending coach).
- The notable organizational coaching change is downstream: Dave Manson became head coach of the AHL San Diego Gulls, with Kelly Buchberger and Steve McCarthy as assistants. This matters for prospect development, but it is not a Ducks bench change.
- Gulls GM Rick Paterson received a multi-year extension.

Sources: [PuckPedia staff listing](https://puckpedia.com/team/anaheim-ducks), [Gulls coaching announcement](https://www.nhl.com/ducks/news/ducks-name-manson-head-coach-of-san-diego-gulls), [Paterson extension](https://www.nhl.com/ducks/news/ducks-extend-contract-of-gulls-gm-paterson), [Quenneville’s 1,000th win](https://www.nhl.com/ducks/news/edmonton-oilers-anaheim-ducks-game-recap-february-25-2026-x4234).

## Ready-to-use copy blocks

### Hero deck

Anaheim’s young core broke a seven-year playoff drought and won a postseason round. Then the Ducks spent the summer paying Leo Carlsson, trading Mason McTavish and Olen Zellweger, and replacing nearly an entire defense corps. Here is the team that finished 2025–26—and the one projected to open 2026–27.

### Carlsson card

**The franchise bet:** Philadelphia forced the decision with a five-year offer sheet worth $90 million. Anaheim matched it, keeping its 21-year-old No. 1 center after a 67-point regular season and an 11-point playoff run.

### Defense card

**Four regulars out:** Jacob Trouba, Radko Gudas, John Carlson and Olen Zellweger all left. Jackson LaCombe and Pavel Mintyukov now anchor a younger group, with Nick Jensen the primary veteran addition and Tristan Luneau pushing for a job.

### McTavish card

**A surprise youth-for-futures trade:** Anaheim moved 23-year-old Mason McTavish after a 41-point season, turning him into two first-round selections. Those picks became Nikita Klepov and, after a small move-up, Marcus Nordmark.

### Unfinished-business card

**Not set yet:** Leading scorer Cutter Gauthier remains unsigned, Troy Terry is recovering from a hip injury, the captaincy is vacant and several roster jobs will be settled in training camp.
